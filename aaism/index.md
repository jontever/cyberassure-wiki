# ISACA AAISM — Advanced in AI Security Management

The **Advanced in AI Security Management™ (AAISM™)** is ISACA's advanced credential for security management professionals responsible for governing, securing, and managing the risk of enterprise AI. It builds on established security management practice and layers on the governance, risk, and technical controls specific to AI systems.

This section maps the full AAISM job practice — the three domains and every sub-topic in the exam content outline — so it can be used as a study reference alongside the rest of this wiki.

## At a glance

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Credential | Advanced in AI Security Management™ (AAISM™) |
| Issuing body | ISACA |
| Prerequisite | Active **CISM** or **CISSP** required to certify |
| Exam | 90 questions, 2 hours |
| Domains | 3 job practice domains |
| Recommended training | ~2 days / ~12 hours |
| CPE | Minimum 10 hours/year in the AI domain (counts toward the standard 20 annual / 120 three-year requirement; no additional three-year requirement) |

> AAISM is an *advanced* credential, not an entry point. Because CISM or CISSP is a hard prerequisite for certification, the exam assumes fluency in general security management and tests how those disciplines extend to AI rather than re-teaching them.

## The three domains

| # | Domain | Weight | Focus |
|---|--------|--------|-------|
| 1 | [AI Governance and Program Management](./domain-1-governance) | 31% | Policy, data governance, program management, and incident response for AI |
| 2 | [AI Risk Management](./domain-2-risk-management) | 31% | Assessing and treating AI risk, threats, vulnerabilities, and supply chain |
| 3 | [AI Technologies and Controls](./domain-3-technologies-controls) | 38% | Security architecture, the AI life cycle, and controls tailored to AI systems |

Domain 3 carries the most weight, reflecting the exam's emphasis on the practical, technical controls that secure AI systems in production.

## Supporting tasks

ISACA defines 22 supporting tasks that span the domains, including:

- Collaborating on the AI governance charter, roles, and responsibilities aligned to business objectives.
- Establishing and maintaining AI-specific security policies, standards, and guidelines.
- Ensuring responsible AI use via leading practice, ethical principles, regulation, and frameworks.
- Participating in the AI risk management life cycle and identifying/assessing the AI threat landscape.
- Designing and implementing testing, vulnerability management, and security architecture for AI.
- Conducting AI impact assessments and ensuring regulatory conformity.
- Embedding and verifying AI security requirements in vendor AI-enabled solutions.
- Inventorying and classifying AI data and assets, and treating data-related risk across the life cycle.
- Running AI-specific incident handling, business continuity, and disaster recovery.
- Defining AI security metrics, conducting human oversight of AI inputs/outputs, and maintaining AI security awareness training.
